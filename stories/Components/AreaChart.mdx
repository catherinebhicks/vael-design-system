# AreaChart

`AreaChart` plots a filled line for trends over a continuous or categorical axis. Use it to show how a value moves over time, or — when `stacked` — how parts combine into a whole across time.

Built on **Highcharts** (styled to the Vael palette) — requires the `highcharts` and `highcharts-react-official` peer dependencies. See [Data Visualization](?path=/docs/foundations-data-visualization--docs) for the chart system and licensing.

## Guidelines

### Do

- Use for trends over time, such as daily active users or request volume across a week.
- Use `stacked` to show part-of-whole over time, e.g. traffic broken down by source.
- Omit `categories` to get a datetime x-axis, and pass `[timestamp, value]` pairs.
- Pass `categories` with plain `number[]` data for evenly spaced, labeled buckets.
- Keep the series count low (roughly 1–4) so the filled areas stay readable.

### Don't

- Don't stack unrelated series whose sum has no meaning — use overlaid lines instead.
- Don't mix time-series pairs and bare numbers in the same chart; pick one data shape.
- Don't rely on fill alone to distinguish overlapping non-stacked series — set explicit `color`s.
- Don't crowd the plot with many thin series; switch to a `LineChart` when comparing precise values.
- Don't hard-code series colors when the default categorical palette already reads clearly.

## Props

| Prop | Type | Default | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| series | `AreaSeries[]` | — | Required. Each series has `name`, `data`, and optional `color`. `data` is `[timestamp, value]` pairs (datetime axis) or `number[]` (categorical axis). |
| categories | `string[]` | — | Category labels for a categorical x-axis. Omit for a datetime axis. |
| stacked | `boolean` | `false` | Stacks series (`stacking: 'normal'`) for part-of-whole-over-time. |
| height | `number` | `320` | Chart height in pixels. |
| className | `string` | — | Applied to the wrapping `div`. |
| style | `React.CSSProperties` | — | Merged onto the wrapper after the theme's chart variables. |

The legend appears automatically when more than one series is provided. Series colors default to the Vael categorical palette; the subtle fill comes from the global theme.

## Accessibility

- The chart is drawn as SVG — pair it with a heading or caption that states what the trend represents, since the shapes alone are not announced.
- Give every series a clear, distinct `name`; these label the legend and any tooltips.
- Don't encode meaning by fill color alone — supply high-contrast `color`s or provide an adjacent data table for screen-reader users.
- For a data-critical view, offer the underlying numbers in an accessible table so the information isn't locked in the visualization.
- Ensure the surrounding container keeps a visible focus outline if the chart is placed inside an interactive card or link.
